## F1 also never ran — a second time-bomb of the same species as B's
Its smoke trained fine and then died at the first checkpoint:
`AttributeError: Can't pickle local object 'main.<locals>._capture_fwd'`.
The diversity hook captured observations by assigning a local closure to
`actor.forward`; `amp_on_policy_runner.save()` calls `export_policy_as_onnx`, whose
`_clone_module` deep-copies the actor **through torch.save**. So any unpicklable
attribute on that module detonates at the first save — after a short smoke that saves
nothing would have passed it.

Fixed by removing the actor patch entirely: `optimizer.step` now samples observations
directly from `alg.storage.observations` (never serialised — the runner saves
state_dicts only). Using a fresh random draw instead of PPO's exact minibatch is fine;
the diversity term is a regulariser over the state distribution, not a per-sample
correction. `tools/test_ase_diversity.py` now round-trips the actor through
`torch.save` both clean and hooked, and asserts that the OLD approach still fails.

Note the deliberate absence of a try/except around the diversity step: a regulariser
that silently switches itself off would let F1 run four hours and be reported as a
diversity experiment with no diversity in it. `ASE/diversity_bonus` in TensorBoard is
the standing proof it stayed on.

## THE COUPLING NOBODY HAD NOTED: AMP obs width and reward scale are not independent
Across the D2 → B → G ladder every added channel makes the discriminator MORE confident
and the style reward WEAKER, monotonically:

| run | AMP obs | amp_loss | style_r | policy_pred | expert_pred |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D2 | 82 | 0.4449 | 0.4419 | 0.3535 | 0.6419 |
| B | 86 | 0.3883 | 0.3846 | 0.3144 | 0.6788 |
| G | 90 | **0.3561** | **0.3551** | 0.2941 | 0.7028 |

ln(2) = 0.693 is the equilibrium where the discriminator cannot separate policy from
expert. All three sit below it and G sits lowest — **the wrong direction**.

The mechanism: slip is 6–13× off reference, an extremely easy feature to separate on.
Handing the discriminator that feature lets it win more cheaply, so the style reward it
emits shrinks, so the policy gets LESS pressure toward the expert. Giving the critic a
better view weakened the learning signal. CLAUDE.md already documents discriminator
saturation as a failure mode; what is new is that **widening the observation can cause
it**, and that the AMP obs and the reward scale therefore cannot be tuned independently.

This is a mechanism hypothesis, not yet a conclusion — the correlation is monotonic
across three runs but causation is not established, and it must be checked against the
matched-condition rollouts. If it holds, the next lever is the reward scale
(`style_weight`, currently 0.5) or a weaker discriminator, NOT abandoning the channel.
"The discriminator can now see the defect" does not imply "the policy will fix it".

## Added 2026-07-29

**ASE diversity objective — DONE** (`DiversityRegularizer` in `training/polyart/ase.py`).
This was the missing third ASE component. It is a term on the POLICY LOSS, not a
reward, so it needed a hook inside rsl_rl's PPO update — which has none between loss
assembly (ppo.py:313) and backward (ppo.py:363). Solution: wrap `optimizer.step`, where
the graph is gone but gradients are live and unstepped, add the diversity gradient by a
second `backward()` (autograd accumulates), then **re-clip the actor** since our
contribution lands after rsl_rl's own clip. Observations are captured off the actor's
forward, keyed on `stochastic_output=True and torch.is_grad_enabled()` — true only
inside `update()`, never during the inference-mode rollout.
Formulation follows nv-tlabs/ASE `ase_agent.py`: `bonus = a_diff/(z_diff+1e-5)`,
`loss = (target - bonus)^2`, coef 0.01, target 1.0, actions clamped to [-1,1].
Verified CPU-only by `tools/test_ase_diversity.py` (12 checks): a z-blind policy scores
bonus 0 and pays the full penalty; 60 steps of training raise spread 0.002 -> 0.79;
a wrong latent slice raises SystemExit. Enabled by `ANIML_DEER_ASE_DIVERSITY=0.01`.
**F1** (= C3 + diversity, single variable) is queued in Q8 behind a 40-iter smoke.

**Leg neglect was largely a MEASUREMENT artifact** — see `docs/LEG_NEGLECT_findings.md`.
1. No limb is kinematically dead in any run: range of motion is 0.79–1.51× expert
   everywhere, including C2's "3 dead legs". They swing; they carry no weight. The
   problem to solve is load, not motion.
2. All 7 rollouts ran with `CMD_ANG_VEL_Z=0.5`, and **the command was never recorded**.
   C3's rollout averaged a sustained +14 °/s LEFT turn, and a cornering deer correctly
   unloads its inside legs. The range is symmetric (±0.5) and each 8 s episode draws
   one command, so a 4-env mean of +0.25 rad/s is ~1.7σ of ordinary sampling luck.
   Every front/hind and left/right number in the earlier tables is provisional.
3. 23–40% of "contacts" sit between 5 N and 20 N on an 809 N animal — grazing touches
   that a duty-threshold score counts as a working leg. Use LOAD SHARE (threshold-free,
   fair share = 0.25), which `tools/leg_kinematics_vs_load.py` now reports.
